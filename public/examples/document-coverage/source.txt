FIELDSTONE EQUIPMENT LIBRARY — FICTIONAL PILOT HANDBOOK
Version: teaching fixture 1.0, October 24, 2026

This is an original fictional teaching document, not the policy of a real organization. All names, fees, dates, procedures, and equipment are invented. It is suitable for a text summarization exercise; it contains no personal records. Section labels are stable source references. Do not infer rules beyond the statements below.

S1. Purpose and scope
The Fieldstone equipment library is planning a six-week pilot for lending small presentation kits to registered community groups. A kit consists of a projector, a remote control, a power lead, and a carrying case. The pilot is intended to help groups hold indoor presentations without purchasing their own equipment. It does not cover outdoor screenings, commercial rentals, or lending individual cables separately. A committee has approved the pilot in principle, but that approval does not establish its opening date. The opening date remains to be announced after the storage review described in S8. Publicity must describe the service as planned until that review is complete. The committee will review pilot demand and returned-kit condition before deciding whether to continue the service. This handbook contains no commitment to a permanent library or an expanded equipment inventory.

S2. Eligible borrowers
A registered community group may request one kit for an indoor event. A named adult representative collects and returns the kit on the group's behalf. Registration means the group's application has been accepted; submitting an application does not by itself establish eligibility. The registration team can confirm the current status, but this document does not specify how long its review takes. Groups must not transfer a borrowed kit to another group. A group may make a new request after returning its current loan and resolving any outstanding return inspection. An eligible group is not guaranteed a kit: stock and the specific requested period must still be confirmed. An individual without an accepted group registration cannot borrow through this pilot. No paid membership tier or priority access scheme is defined here.

S3. Requests and confirmation
Send a request at least five calendar days before the desired collection date. State the group reference, collection date, event date, and intended indoor use. The request is provisional until the desk sends written confirmation naming the collection and return dates. An acknowledgment that the request was received is not confirmation. The desk may ask for clarification when a date or use is missing. Do not announce that equipment has been booked while clarification is outstanding. Requests made with fewer than five calendar days' notice are normally declined, with one narrow exception: an accepted group replacing its own failed projector may be considered under the emergency process in S9. That exception does not waive stock checks or written confirmation. This handbook supplies no live stock calendar and makes no promise about a particular date.

S4. Collection and return windows
The ordinary desk window is Tuesday and Thursday, 16:00–18:00 local time. The desk is closed on public holidays even if a holiday falls on one of those days. Collect only within the window recorded in the written confirmation. Staff check the kit contents with the representative and record any visible pre-existing damage. The ordinary loan period is three calendar days from collection, but the written confirmation must state the actual return appointment. If the calculated return day is a public holiday or a day when the desk is closed, staff arrange a return appointment before confirming the loan; borrowers must not choose their own extension. The document does not specify a time zone identifier or a holiday calendar. Confirm those locally rather than converting a time from this fictional handbook into a real appointment.

S5. Deposit, charges, and money not specified
A refundable deposit of 40 credits is required at collection. Credits are fictional units used only in this exercise. The deposit is held until the kit's return inspection is complete; return of the deposit is not promised at the instant the kit is handed back. The handbook does not set a rental charge, a late charge, a damage charge, or a replacement price. Absence of a listed charge does not establish that lending is free, and the deposit must not be described as the total cost. The desk must explain applicable charges before the borrower accepts the confirmed arrangement. For the pilot report, staff record the deposit separately from any other amount. Borrowers should ask about unresolved financial terms before collection rather than assume that a requested date is fully agreed.

S6. Use and care during a loan
Use the projector indoors on a stable surface with its vents unobstructed. Keep the carrying case, remote control, and lead together so every component can be checked on return. Do not repair, modify, or lend the kit onward. If the equipment stops working, stop using it and contact the desk; the handbook does not authorize opening the case or substituting an unknown power supply. The representative remains the desk's contact even when another member operates the projector. The pilot does not provide a technician at the event and does not promise compatibility with every laptop, adapter, or presentation format. Groups should ask about compatibility before confirmation and allow time for a basic check at collection. A successful collection check cannot establish that every later presentation will run without problems.

S7. Return inspection and missing items
Bring all four kit components to the agreed return appointment. Staff compare the contents with the collection record and note new damage or missing items. A kit handed to an unrelated volunteer outside desk hours has not completed the return process. The deposit review follows inspection, including any unresolved discrepancy. The handbook does not specify a number of days for refund processing. If the representative disputes an inspection note, the desk records the disagreement and asks the pilot coordinator to review it; this does not authorize an immediate deduction or establish a particular charge. Groups must retain their confirmation and collection record until the return issue is resolved. The pilot report should count unresolved inspections separately from completed returns rather than label every physically received kit as a completed loan.

S8. Opening dependency and capacity
There are two kits in the planned inventory. Before lending begins, the facilities team must confirm a lockable storage area and the coordinator must approve the collection checklist. The facilities review is pending in this version of the handbook. The collection checklist is drafted but not approved. Therefore neither condition for opening has been completed merely because the committee approved the pilot. The coordinator may publish an opening announcement only after both conditions are satisfied. Two kits in inventory do not mean two kits are available on every requested date: a kit may be on loan or awaiting inspection. This handbook contains no booking ledger. Requests for simultaneous events must be checked against the actual confirmed schedule after opening. Do not turn planned capacity into a promise that a specific group can borrow immediately.

S9. Emergency replacement exception
An accepted group whose own projector fails may ask for emergency replacement with less than five calendar days' notice. The coordinator must explicitly approve the exception, and a kit must be available for the requested period. Written confirmation is still required. This exception applies only to the request lead time. It does not permit collection outside the agreed desk window, waive the deposit, extend the ordinary loan automatically, or remove the opening requirements in S8. A first-time applicant awaiting registration approval cannot use the exception to bypass S2. If approval or stock cannot be established, record the request as unresolved or declined, not booked. The handbook provides no emergency phone number and no promise of same-day service. Do not invent a contact route or a guaranteed response time in a summary.

S10. Reporting, changes, and unresolved questions
For the pilot review, record confirmed loans, completed returns, unresolved inspections, and requests declined because stock was unavailable. Record emergency approvals separately so reviewers can distinguish them from ordinary requests. The coordinator owns the current handbook version. A discussion note or suggested change does not amend a rule until the coordinator publishes a revised version. If a summary conflicts with the handbook, return to the relevant section and ask for clarification; the summary does not override the source. Open questions in this version include the opening date, actual date-specific availability, applicable charges beyond the deposit, refund timing, the local holiday calendar, and a contact address. A useful public summary should preserve those gaps. It should also say that this is a fictional pilot document, rather than presenting its procedures as advice from a real lending service.
